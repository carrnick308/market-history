Returns

Every month since 1926, grouped by how concentrated the market was, split into thirds of history. Each row shows the average yearly return over the look-ahead period for the biggest 10% of companies and for the other 90%, and how often the rest came out ahead. Neighbouring months share most of their history, so the high group rests on only a few separate episodes.

The same groups, for the whole US share market, compared with leaving the money in cash (US Treasury bills, the yardstick for cash; the data starts in 1934, so the cash columns cover start months from 1934). “Periods that lost money” is the share of start months where the shares were worth less at the end than at the start.

Concentration rarely comes alone. High concentration has often coincided with high prices (around 2000, and in the 2020s) or a crash and a war (the 1930s and early 1940s). These results can’t show that concentration itself caused what followed, so read them alongside the P/E and the episodes below.

About these numbers

  • Data: Eugene F. Fama and Kenneth R. French, Portfolios Formed on Size, from the Kenneth R. French Data Library, built from the CRSP database. Inflation: US CPI from Robert J. Shiller’s data (shillerdata.com).
  • “The biggest 10% of companies” is the top size group among all US-listed shares (NYSE, AMEX and NASDAQ), using the size cut-offs of NYSE companies and re-sorted each June. It is a set share of companies rather than a fixed number: It rises and falls with the more familiar “top 10 companies” measure, which also reached record highs in 1932 and 2025.
  • Returns are value-weighted (bigger companies count for more), with dividends reinvested, in US dollars, with no fees or tax. “The rest” includes small companies that can cost more to invest in. NZ dollar figures aren’t shown because they only start in 1985, which would leave too few episodes.
  • Low, average and high are thirds of all months since 1926 (). An episode is a stretch of high months, allowing gaps of up to a year.
  • History only holds a few episodes of high concentration, and neighbouring start months share most of their history, so treat the percentages as a guide rather than precise odds.
